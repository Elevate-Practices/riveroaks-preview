What Is Considered a Dental Emergency?

A dental emergency is generally a problem involving significant pain, trauma, swelling, infection, bleeding, or damage that may worsen without prompt attention.

Common reasons to seek urgent dental care include:

  • Severe or persistent toothache
  • Broken or chipped tooth
  • Knocked-out tooth
  • Dental abscess or swelling
  • Lost filling or crown
  • Cracked tooth
  • Dental trauma
  • Pain after recent dental treatment
  • Uncontrolled oral bleeding
  • Signs of infection

If you are unsure whether your condition is urgent, call our office. Our team can help determine whether a same-day appointment may be appropriate.

Common Dental Emergencies We Evaluate

Severe Toothache

Persistent tooth pain may be caused by decay, infection, a cracked tooth, gum problems, or irritation of the dental pulp. The goal of an emergency evaluation is to identify the cause rather than simply mask the symptoms.

Our general dentistry services support diagnosis and treatment planning for a wide range of urgent dental concerns.

Broken or Chipped Tooth

A broken tooth may range from a small enamel chip to a deeper fracture involving the inner tooth structure. Treatment depends on the extent of damage and may involve smoothing, bonding, a filling, a crown, or another restorative option.

If the tooth needs additional protection, dental crowns may be considered when clinically appropriate.

Knocked-Out Tooth

A knocked-out permanent tooth is time-sensitive. Handle the tooth by the crown rather than the root, rinse it gently if it is dirty, and avoid scrubbing or removing attached tissue.

If possible, place the tooth back in the socket without forcing it. If that is not possible, keep it moist in milk or a tooth-preservation solution and seek dental care promptly.

Reimplantation is not always possible, but faster evaluation may improve treatment options.

Lost Filling or Crown

A missing filling or crown can leave the underlying tooth sensitive or vulnerable to additional damage. Avoid chewing on that side and contact the office for an evaluation.

Depending on the condition of the tooth, treatment may involve replacing the restoration or using another repair option such as composite fillings.

Dental Abscess or Infection

Dental infections may cause throbbing pain, swelling, tenderness, fever, drainage, or a bad taste in the mouth. These symptoms should be evaluated promptly because untreated infections can spread.

Depending on the source of infection, treatment may involve drainage, medication when appropriate, root canal treatment, extraction, or referral. Learn more about endodontics for problems involving the inner portion of a tooth.

How Same-Day Emergency Appointments Work

1. Call the Office

Contact River Oaks Family Dental at (817) 738-4141 and describe your symptoms as clearly as possible.

Helpful information includes:

  • When the problem started
  • Location of the pain
  • Whether swelling is present
  • Whether a tooth is broken or missing
  • Whether there has been trauma
  • Whether you have fever or other symptoms
  • Whether the problem involves a recent filling, crown, or other treatment

Same-day appointments may be available depending on the schedule, time of the call, and urgency of the condition.

2. Emergency Examination

During your visit, the dentist will examine the affected area and review your symptoms. Digital X-rays may be used when clinically appropriate to identify decay, fractures, infection, or other problems that are not visible during the exam.

3. Immediate Treatment or Stabilization

Some emergencies can be treated during the same visit, while others may require temporary stabilization followed by additional care.

Possible treatment may include:

  • Filling or restoration
  • Dental crown
  • Root canal therapy
  • Tooth extraction
  • Temporary repair
  • Infection management
  • Pain management
  • Referral to a specialist or hospital when appropriate

4. Follow-Up Care

If your emergency visit resolves the immediate problem but additional treatment is needed, our team will explain the next steps and help coordinate follow-up care.

How Quickly Can You Be Seen?

We offer same-day emergency appointments when availability allows, but the exact timing depends on several factors.

These may include:

  • How urgent the condition is
  • What time you contact the office
  • The treatment required
  • Existing scheduled appointments
  • Whether diagnostic imaging or additional procedures are needed

Calling earlier in the day may provide more scheduling options, but same-day treatment cannot be guaranteed in every situation.

Taking the right steps before your appointment may help protect the affected area and reduce discomfort.

For a Toothache

  • Rinse gently with warm water
  • Floss carefully if food may be trapped
  • Use a cold compress on the outside of the face if swelling is present
  • Do not place aspirin directly on the gums or tooth

For a Knocked-Out Tooth

  • Handle the tooth by the crown
  • Rinse gently if needed
  • Do not scrub the root
  • Keep the tooth moist
  • Seek dental care as quickly as possible

For a Broken Tooth

  • Save any broken pieces if possible
  • Rinse your mouth gently
  • Apply a cold compress if swelling develops
  • Avoid chewing on the affected side

For a Lost Crown or Filling

  • Keep the area clean
  • Avoid hard or sticky foods
  • Do not use household glue or non-dental adhesives
  • Contact the office for guidance

For Oral Bleeding

  • Apply gentle pressure with clean gauze
  • Seek prompt care if bleeding does not stop

When to Go to the Emergency Room Instead

Some situations require hospital emergency care rather than a dental office.

Call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department if you experience:

  • Difficulty breathing
  • Difficulty swallowing
  • Rapidly spreading swelling of the face or neck
  • Uncontrolled bleeding
  • Serious facial or head trauma
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Symptoms suggesting a medical emergency

A hospital can address airway concerns, severe trauma, systemic infection, or other life-threatening conditions. Once the medical emergency is stabilized, dental follow-up may still be needed.

Risks of Delaying Emergency Dental Treatment

Some dental problems become more difficult to treat when care is delayed.

Potential consequences may include:

  • Increasing pain
  • Spread of infection
  • Additional tooth damage
  • Greater risk of fracture
  • Need for more complex treatment
  • Possible tooth loss in some cases

The exact risk depends on the condition, which is why prompt evaluation is important when symptoms are severe or worsening.

Who May Need a Different Level of Care?

Some patients may require a hospital, oral surgeon, endodontist, or another specialist instead of routine in-office emergency treatment.

Examples include:

  • Severe facial trauma
  • Airway compromise
  • Rapidly spreading facial or neck swelling
  • Uncontrolled bleeding
  • Complex surgical injuries
  • Conditions requiring treatment outside the scope of a general dental office

When appropriate, our team can help explain the next level of care.

Same-Day Emergency Dentist vs. Hospital Emergency Room

A dental office and hospital emergency room serve different purposes.

Emergency Dentist

An emergency dentist can evaluate tooth-specific problems, take dental X-rays, diagnose dental infections or fractures, and provide procedures such as fillings, crowns, root canal therapy, or extraction when appropriate.

Hospital Emergency Room

A hospital is the appropriate setting for medical emergencies such as breathing difficulty, severe trauma, uncontrolled bleeding, or rapidly spreading infection. Emergency departments may stabilize pain or infection, but they may not provide definitive dental treatment.

Same-Day Emergency Appointments

Same-Day Emergency Appointments, answered.

What dental problems need a same-day emergency appointment?

Severe tooth pain, swelling, a suspected infection, a broken or knocked-out tooth, uncontrolled oral bleeding, or a lost restoration causing significant symptoms may require prompt evaluation. If you are unsure whether the problem is urgent, call the office and describe your symptoms so the dental team can help determine the appropriate next step.

How much does an emergency dental visit cost in River Oaks, TX?

Emergency dental costs vary based on the examination, X-rays, diagnosis, and treatment required. A simple restoration generally differs in cost from a root canal, crown, or extraction. After evaluating the problem, the dental team can explain recommended treatment and provide a more specific estimate.

Can a same-day emergency dentist treat a broken tooth or severe toothache?

In many cases, yes. Treatment depends on the diagnosis and schedule availability. A broken tooth may require a filling, crown, root canal, extraction, or temporary stabilization. Severe tooth pain may be caused by decay, infection, fracture, or another condition that must be identified before the appropriate treatment is selected.

What should I do if my tooth is knocked out?

Handle the tooth by the crown, not the root. Rinse it gently if needed, but do not scrub it. If possible, reposition it in the socket without forcing it. Otherwise, keep it moist in milk or a tooth-preservation solution and seek dental care promptly.

When should I go to the emergency room instead of an emergency dentist?

Go to the emergency room or call 911 for difficulty breathing or swallowing, rapidly spreading facial or neck swelling, uncontrolled bleeding, serious facial trauma, loss of consciousness, or other potentially life-threatening symptoms. Dental follow-up may still be needed after the immediate medical emergency is stabilized.
